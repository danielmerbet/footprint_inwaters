How the oscillation footprint F is computed

The footprint is the fraction of a water body's year-to-year temperature swings that the three big climate oscillations organise. Here it is, computed step by step on real simulated data — pick a site and watch the number build.

STEP 01

Start with the raw temperature record

Each grid cell has one annual water-temperature value per year, 1950–2021 (72 years). Most of the long-term change is a slow warming trend — not what we're after. We want the fast, year-to-year wobble the oscillations drive, so the trend has to come out first.

STEP 02

Detrend to isolate the interannual signal

Subtract the straight-line trend. What's left is the interannual anomaly T′ — each year's departure from where the trend says it "should" be. This is the quantity the footprint explains. Detrending matters: some indices carry their own warming trend, and without this step a shared trend masquerades as a real teleconnection.

Interannual anomaly T′ (°C)
T′ anomaly
Positive = warmer than the trend that year; negative = cooler. Mean zero by construction.
STEP 03

Line up the three climate drivers

Each oscillation is summarised by one index, taken in the season it peaks — ENSO in boreal winter (DJF), NAO in winter (DJFM), IOD in autumn (SON). All three are detrended and standardised to mean 0, SD 1, so their regression coefficients are directly comparable.

Standardised oscillation indices
ENSO IOD NAO
Same three series at every site — what differs between sites is how strongly the water tracks them.
STEP 04

Fit them together, and see what the model reproduces

Regress the anomaly T′ on the three indices at once. The fitted line is βE·ENSO + βI·IOD + βN·NAO — the best additive reconstruction of the water's wobble from the oscillations. Toggle a driver off in the panel above to watch its contribution leave the fit.

STEP 05

The footprint F is the variance the model captures

F is the adjusted R² of that fit — the share of interannual temperature variance the three oscillations jointly explain, adjusted so that adding a useless predictor can't inflate it. A cell is called teleconnected when F is field-significant after false-discovery-rate control.

STEP 06

Every cell on Earth gets an F — that's the map

Run steps 1–5 for all ~100,000 river and lake cells and you have the global footprint. The four example sites already span the range: a textbook ENSO cell, the Amazon, a modest North-Atlantic responder, and a cell where the oscillations explain essentially nothing.
